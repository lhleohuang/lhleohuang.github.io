Lianghuan Huang

I'm a researcher working at the intersection of AI and neuroscience. Currently I do research at Mila - Quebec AI Institute, where I am advised by Prof. Shahab Bakhtiari. Most broadly, I am interested in building AIs that help humans flourish. That means AIs that respect who we are, as humans, and, conditioning on that, become our friends. To achieve this, we must develop a scientific understanding of ourselves from the perspective of learning, and design AIs that respect or build upon that understanding. To illustrate the urgency of this issue: if robots will become an integral part of our daily lives, we do not want them to manipulate us like some current technologies do ;)

More concretely, my research interests lie in self-supervised learning. I’m interested in how AI systems learn representations that capture the underlying structure and dynamics of their environment, and how learning objectives, architectures, and data shape the information these representations preserve. I explore these questions through theoretical analysis and controlled experiments, often taking inspiration from neuroscience, with the ultimate goal of learning representations that support real-world action, planning, and generalization.

Previously, I graduated with a physics major from the University of Pennsylvania, working with Profs. Konrad Kording and Osbert Bastani.

Past Projects

Formalizing the Binding Problem

How do Vision Transformers understand scenes with multiple objects without confusing their features? We formalized feature-to-object "binding" with an information-theoretic approach and devised a probing method to quantify binding information in the representations of any artificial model. Our work has important implications for object-centric perception in visual reasoning and embodied AI. Published at ICML 2026.
